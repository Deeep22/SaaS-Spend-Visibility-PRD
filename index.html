01

The Problem

Mid-market companies — 200 to 2,000 employees — accumulate SaaS subscriptions faster than any single team can track. Department heads buy tools independently to solve immediate needs. Finance discovers the spend months later, buried across a dozen cost centers. IT discovers the security exposure only after an audit, or an incident. Nobody owns the full picture — because nobody was ever assigned to.

Wasted spend

Duplicate tools across departments, unused seats, and forgotten renewals nobody actively decided to keep.

Security & compliance blind spots

No full inventory of what holds company data, who has access, or what meets compliance requirements.

No clear ownership

When a contract comes up for renewal, there's often no one who can confirm it's still needed — so it renews by default.

02

Who Feels This

Three people, three different relationships to the same problem.

Maya Chen
Director of IT Operations · Brightline Logistics (850 employees, Series C)
Champion

Inherited SaaS oversight as a side responsibility eight months ago, after Finance flagged a budget overrun. Her only tool is a shared spreadsheet that's perpetually out of date. Her job isn't "manage SaaS" — it's "don't get blindsided again."

  • Sales paid for two competing outreach tools for 14 months — two reps each bought one, neither knew about the other.
  • A security review found 11 apps with customer-database access that IT never approved or reviewed.
  • A $40K analytics tool auto-renewed three weeks before anyone realized the team that requested it had churned entirely.
Diego Ramirez
VP of Finance
Reports up

Owns the software budget and reports it to the board. His pain isn't sprawl itself — it's that spend is scattered across a dozen expense categories with no consistent tagging, so every quarter he has to manually reconcile numbers before reporting with any confidence.

  • A board member asked about YoY software spend growth, and he didn't have a clean answer on the spot.
  • Wants accurate, real-time spend visibility he can trust — without chasing people down every quarter.
Priya Patel
Senior Marketing Manager
Actual buyer

The person who causes the sprawl — not from carelessness, but because she's judged on campaign velocity, and waiting on procurement can cost her a launch window. She's bought three tools this year without looping in IT, because nobody told her she needed to.

  • That a tool like this becomes "SaaS police" — something that slows her down or takes away tools she relies on.
  • That doing her job well starts to feel like being surveilled.

Maya and Diego want visibility and control. Priya wants speed and autonomy. Designing purely for Maya's needs builds something Priya routes around — which is exactly how shadow IT happens in the first place.

03

Goals & Success Metrics

  1. Give Maya and Diego continuous, automatic visibility into SaaS spend and usage — without manual spreadsheet-chasing.
  2. Catch and prevent waste before money is spent, not in a quarterly post-mortem.
  3. Do both without slowing down or feeling adversarial to buyers like Priya — governance as a safety net, not a checkpoint.
North Star Metric
% of total company SaaS spend that is visible, actively reviewed, and intentionally retained — vs. unmonitored or auto-renewed by default.

Primary

  • Recovered spend (target: 15–20% within 2 quarters)
  • Renewal miss rate (target: trending to 0%)
  • Reporting time: days → minutes

Secondary

  • Coverage: % of total spend actually visible
  • Action rate on flagged duplicates/unused tools
  • Weekly active usage by IT/Finance

Guardrail

  • Time-to-approval holds steady or drops
  • Buyer sentiment: helpful vs. obstructive
  • Shadow purchase rate falls — because the sanctioned path is easier, not stricter

Requirements

Six epics, traced back to a persona pain point or a named edge case.

🔍

No matching epics found.

US-1.1

As Maya, I want to connect our SSO/IdP so every app employees log into is automatically discovered.

  • OAuth to Okta and Google Workspace at launch
  • Each app shows last login date + active user count
  • Apps with no spend match flagged "discovered, cost unknown"
US-1.2

As Maya, I want a connected card/expense feed (or CSV upload), so spend-only purchases are captured even without SSO.

  • Matches transactions to existing apps by vendor name
  • Unmatched spend creates an entry tagged "spend confirmed, usage unknown"
US-2.1

As Diego, I want spend broken down by department, cost center, and app, so I can report accurately without manual reconciliation.

  • Currency normalization across multi-currency spend
  • Filterable by period, department, category
  • One-click export to CSV/PDF
US-2.2

As Diego, I want spend trend views, so I catch anomalies before they become a surprise.

  • MoM/QoQ trend with % change
  • Auto-alert when any app's spend jumps >20% MoM
US-3.1

As Maya, I want every contract on a shared calendar with configurable alerts, so nothing auto-renews without a decision.

  • Default 30/14/7-day alerts, configurable per contract
  • Can't mark "reviewed" without a logged decision: renew / cancel / renegotiate
  • Missed deadline escalates to owner + Maya
US-4.1

As Maya, I want tools in the same category flagged, so I can investigate consolidation.

  • Minimum taxonomy: CRM, sales engagement, analytics, comms, design, dev tools, HR
  • Flags show estimated overlap savings
  • API-only/no-login tools excluded from "unused" flags
US-5.1

As Priya, I want to register a new tool in under 2 minutes, with no approval gate blocking my purchase.

  • Form requires only: tool name, purpose, est. cost, department
  • Submission routes an FYI — not an approval request — to Maya
  • Confirmation copy reinforces "register, don't ask permission"
US-6.1

As Maya, I want every app to have a business owner, so decisions never default to "nobody knows."

  • Supports multiple owners per app (e.g., a CRM shared by Sales + Marketing)
  • HRIS offboarding event triggers reassignment prompt
  • Unowned apps flagged on the dashboard as a risk indicator

Security

Read-only scoped integrations; encrypted at rest/in transit; SOC 2 Type II is a launch requirement.

Roles

Minimum three: Admin (Maya), Finance Viewer (Diego), Requester (Priya) — each sees only what's relevant.

Performance

Dashboard loads in <3 seconds up to ~500 tracked apps.

Reliability

A failed sync alerts Admins — a stale dashboard is worse than a known gap.

Open Questions

  • Open

    Pricing model — per tracked app, flat platform fee, or % of identified savings? A savings-% model requires customers to trust our calculations a lot more.

  • Open

    Cross-department visibility — should Priya see what other departments spend, or only her own? A real org-sensitivity question, not just a permissions toggle.

  • Open

    Disagreement resolution — when Maya and a department head disagree on cancelling a flagged tool, is there an escalation path, or does the tool stay neutral?

  • Open

    Compliance review — does aggregating company financial and access data require its own DPA/security track beyond our own SOC 2?

  • Open

    Build vs. integrate — deep integrations with Ramp/Brex/Expensify, or start with CSV upload and treat integrations as a paid expansion?
